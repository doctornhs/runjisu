§f9360d4 Method and<br>FAQ
§a8a2551 Here is where every number on this site comes from. It is built and reviewed by an orthopedic surgeon who also loves to run.
§e0b6ed9 Why we built it
§5b8e0f8 Running is the means; the goal is a body that walks and moves well for a long time. Many running injuries start from running faster than your fitness allows or longer than your preparation supports.
§7414de3 So the tools here do two things: tell you the pace that fits your current fitness, and make sure distance and gear don't get ahead of your body.
§a776c33 The {{brand}} formula
§c5210d6 We use two equations published by exercise physiologists Jack Daniels and Jimmy Gilbert in 1979. The first gives the oxygen cost of a running speed; the second gives how long that intensity can be sustained. Dividing one by the other gives {{brand}}.
§658be0c Oxygen cost = −4.60 + 0.182258·v + 0.000104·v² (v: meters per minute) Sustained fraction = 0.8 + 0.1894393·e^(−0.012778·t) + 0.2989558·e^(−0.1932605·t) (t: minutes) {{brand}} = oxygen cost ÷ sustained fraction
§6d471e8 Intensity
§b8c6ef6 How it's calculated
§ea1d05b E Easy
§f50fe0a Speed at 62–70% of {{brand}} (inside Daniels' E range of 59–74%)
§906c101 M Marathon
§8ca332b Predicted marathon pace from the same {{brand}}
§cab4b7a T Threshold
§cbf44c7 88% of {{brand}}
§8c8d2e6 I Interval
§7483c23 97.5% of {{brand}}
§af11a09 R Repetition
§d595c1a 6 seconds per 400 m faster than I pace
§bae987a Errors can grow for results shorter than about 3:30 or longer than 4 hours. We calculate directly from the published equations rather than copying the book's tables, so results may differ from the tables by 1–2 seconds.
§8a4acd2 Fitness age formulas
§c96db01 12-minute run VO2max = (distance in m − 504.9) ÷ 44.73 1.6 km walk VO2max = 132.853 − 0.0769·weight (lb) − 0.3877·age + 6.315·(male 1, female 0) − 3.2649·time (min) − 0.1565·heart rate at finish
§8de2b5e Fitness age is found by matching your estimate to the median of each age group in the FRIEND data and interpolating between groups.

§ae2ce92 Frequently asked questions
§2ed2e7d Is {{brand}} the same as VO2max?
§192bb05 Same units, different values. VO2max is measured in a lab from your breathing gases; {{brand}} is worked backward from a race result. Runners who are more economical get a {{brand}} above their measured VO2max, while recreational runners usually get one below it. For setting training paces, {{brand}} is the more useful number.
§1e585cb My peer ranking looks lower than I expected.
§8e51bfd The math is right; it's a limit of the comparison. The peer table is lab-measured VO2max, and values from race results tend to come out lower. Marathon results also mix in endurance and fueling, which widens the gap. That's why we show rankings as "top X% or better", meaning at least this good. A hard 5K or 10K, or a 12-minute run, gets you closer to the true value.
§c76bcaa My E pace feels too slow.
§c9248d4 That's normal. Easy running works through time on your feet, not speed. Running it faster doesn't add benefit; it only slows recovery. If you can still hold a conversation, you're fine.
§440de87 My marathon was much slower than predicted.
§d0d2358 Predictions only reflect aerobic capacity. They can't account for the endurance to hold on after 30 km, fueling, weather or hills. Try recalculating from a half marathon result, or ask whether your long runs were enough.
§6d8c712 What if I don't have a race result?
§b19e057 Run 3 km or 5 km on a track or flat road at an even, all-out effort and enter that. A jogging time will give a {{brand}} lower than your real one.
§dc52c1a Should I follow the weekly plan exactly?
§23250b8 The plan is a sample week matched to your weekly distance and paces. It doesn't know how many weeks you have until race day or how you've run in recent months. Use it as a frame and adjust it downward to match how your body feels.
§6cfe245 Where are my entries stored?
§70de8f5 Only in the browser on this device. Nothing is sent to a server. Clearing browser data erases it, so copy a backup from the run log screen.
§82a2375 Can I use it like an app?
§e938c12 Add it to your home screen and it opens like an app; after the first visit it calculates even offline. On iPhone Safari, tap Share then Add to Home Screen; on Android Chrome, open the menu and choose Install app or Add to Home screen.
§14d876b Please note
§e5d0bbe The calculations and explanations on this site are general information and do not replace diagnosis or treatment. If you have pain or a heart or joint condition, talk to a medical professional before raising your training intensity.

§a1b8d81 Why fitness age matters
§9dbaf40 A Cleveland Clinic study that followed 122,007 adults after treadmill stress tests found that the fitter the group, the lower the risk of death, with no upper limit to the benefit. The fittest group had an 80% lower risk of death than the least fit (bottom 25%).
§105f439 It's an observational study, so it doesn't mean raising your fitness adds that many years. But unlike age or family history, aerobic fitness is something training can change.
§344b4bd Keeping a body that walks and moves well for a long time is the goal, and running is one of the easiest ways to get there.
§76ef6b3 To lower your fitness age
§41dc6ef Frequency first. Three or more times a week, 30 minutes at a conversational pace is the starting point. Mixing walking and running is fine.
§b58eba4 After a few months, add a weekly session with a few 3–5 minute efforts that leave you breathing hard. It's the most efficient zone for raising aerobic capacity.
§f010c40 Retest the same way every 8–12 weeks to see the change.

§c4c3be1 Before you read the results
§dab1328 The comparison uses treadmill stress-test data from 7,783 US adults (FRIEND, 2015). Values for other populations may differ.
§745a7e5 Values from race results tend to come out lower than lab VO2max. A study comparing 5 km results with measured values found both collegiate and recreational runners came out lower, with a bigger gap in recreational runners. So read fitness age and ranking from race results as "at least this good", especially from half or full marathon results.
§fb12750 Race results and the 12-minute run only work when you give it everything. An easy-effort time will make your fitness age look older than it is.
§73bc124 The 1.6 km walk equation was built for adults aged 30–69 and has an error of about 4–6. Experienced runners may score low on the walk test, so use a race result instead.
§5473709 If you have chest pain, severe shortness of breath or dizziness, or a heart condition, talk to a medical professional before doing an all-out test.
