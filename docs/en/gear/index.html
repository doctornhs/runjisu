Pick the type
before the product

We don't recommend specific products. We only cover which type suits which runner and what to look for. For shoes, the most reliable test so far is still what feels comfortable when you try them on.

Daily cushioned shoes

For
The first pair for any runner who runs three or more times a week, mostly at E pace.
How to choose
Try them on in the evening when your feet are swollen. Leave a fingernail's width in front of your big toe, and your heel shouldn't lift.
See cushioned running shoes on Coupang

Stability shoes

For
Runners whose ankles roll inward a lot on landing and whose shoes wear fastest on the inner sole.
Good to know
Choosing stability shoes by arch shape alone has weak evidence for preventing injury. Still, a randomized trial found fewer injuries in runners whose feet roll inward, so they're worth considering.
See stability shoes on Coupang

Lightweight and racing shoes

For
Runners looking for a second pair for T, I and R sessions and races.
Good to know
They wear out sooner and change the load on your calves and Achilles. Get used to them on short sessions first.
See lightweight racing shoes on Coupang

Running watches and heart-rate monitors

For
Runners who want a record of distance and pace. The distance spike check and shoe mileage are only accurate with a log.
How to choose
Check GPS accuracy and battery life first. For sessions where heart rate changes quickly, like intervals, a chest strap is more accurate than a wrist sensor.
See running watches on Coupang

Foam rollers and massage balls

For
Runners whose calves and thighs feel tight after runs.
Good to know
They help short-term with tightness and flexibility. Evidence that they prevent injury is not clear. Don't press hard on bone or on a single painful spot.
See foam rollers on Coupang

Race-day accessories

For
Runners preparing for a half marathon or longer: race bib belts, energy gels, running belts or vests.
Good to know
Use nothing new on race day. Try gels on long runs first and choose one that sits well in your stomach.
See race bib belts on Coupang

Why there's no buy button for knee and ankle braces

Braces don't fix the cause of pain. They're a short-term tool when some instability lingers as you return after a diagnosis; if you can't run without one for more than two weeks, see a doctor first. Some braces are classified as medical devices, so this doctor-made site doesn't link to purchases.
