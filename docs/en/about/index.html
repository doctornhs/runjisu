Here is where every number on this site comes from. It is built and reviewed by an orthopedic surgeon who also loves to run.

Why we built it

Running is the means; the goal is a body that walks and moves well for a long time. Many running injuries start from running faster than your fitness allows or longer than your preparation supports.

So the tools here do two things: tell you the pace that fits your current fitness, and make sure distance and gear don't get ahead of your body.

The Runjisu formula

We use two equations published by exercise physiologists Jack Daniels and Jimmy Gilbert in 1979. The first gives the oxygen cost of a running speed; the second gives how long that intensity can be sustained. Dividing one by the other gives Runjisu.

Oxygen cost = −4.60 + 0.182258·v + 0.000104·v² (v: meters per minute) Sustained fraction = 0.8 + 0.1894393·e^(−0.012778·t) + 0.2989558·e^(−0.1932605·t) (t: minutes) Runjisu = oxygen cost ÷ sustained fraction

Errors can grow for results shorter than about 3:30 or longer than 4 hours. We calculate directly from the published equations rather than copying the book's tables, so results may differ from the tables by 1–2 seconds.

Fitness age formulas

12-minute run VO2max = (distance in m − 504.9) ÷ 44.73 1.6 km walk VO2max = 132.853 − 0.0769·weight (lb) − 0.3877·age + 6.315·(male 1, female 0) − 3.2649·time (min) − 0.1565·heart rate at finish

Fitness age is found by matching your estimate to the median of each age group in the FRIEND data and interpolating between groups.

Frequently asked questions

Is Runjisu the same as VO2max?

Same units, different values. VO2max is measured in a lab from your breathing gases; Runjisu is worked backward from a race result. Runners who are more economical get a Runjisu above their measured VO2max, while recreational runners usually get one below it. For setting training paces, Runjisu is the more useful number.

My peer ranking looks lower than I expected.

The math is right; it's a limit of the comparison. The peer table is lab-measured VO2max, and values from race results tend to come out lower. Marathon results also mix in endurance and fueling, which widens the gap. That's why we show rankings as "top X% or better", meaning at least this good. A hard 5K or 10K, or a 12-minute run, gets you closer to the true value.

My E pace feels too slow.

That's normal. Easy running works through time on your feet, not speed. Running it faster doesn't add benefit; it only slows recovery. If you can still hold a conversation, you're fine.

My marathon was much slower than predicted.

Predictions only reflect aerobic capacity. They can't account for the endurance to hold on after 30 km, fueling, weather or hills. Try recalculating from a half marathon result, or ask whether your long runs were enough.

What if I don't have a race result?

Run 3 km or 5 km on a track or flat road at an even, all-out effort and enter that. A jogging time will give a Runjisu lower than your real one.

Should I follow the weekly plan exactly?

The plan is a sample week matched to your weekly distance and paces. It doesn't know how many weeks you have until race day or how you've run in recent months. Use it as a frame and adjust it downward to match how your body feels.

Where are my entries stored?

Only in the browser on this device. Nothing is sent to a server. Clearing browser data erases it, so copy a backup from the run log screen.

Can I use it like an app?

Add it to your home screen and it opens like an app; after the first visit it calculates even offline. On iPhone Safari, tap Share then Add to Home Screen; on Android Chrome, open the menu and choose Install app or Add to Home screen.
