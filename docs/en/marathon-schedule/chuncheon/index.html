Course

  • A loop course that starts at Gongjicheon and circles Uiam Lake. It turns near Sinmae Bridge at about 21 km, then passes Seosang Bridge and Chuncheon Dam on the way back to Gongjicheon.
  • There is a gentle climb right after the start, and the lakeside stretch from 5 to 15 km is mostly flat. Climbs cluster around Seosang Bridge and Chuncheon Dam at 25–30 km, the course's biggest test.

Injury notes from an orthopedic surgeon

  • The deciding stretch is the 25–30 km climb, so if you push the pace on the flat lakeside early, your legs won't hold up late. Run the first half slightly slower than your splits.
  • Starting about four weeks out, add hill repeats to prepare the calf and glute muscles used on climbs. Uphill, shorten your stride and stay upright.
  • Late-October mornings in Chuncheon are chilly, so your muscles cool quickly while waiting to start.
  • When preparing for a marathon, injuries happen in training more than on race day. A cohort study found overuse injuries rise when a single run goes more than 10% beyond your longest run of the last 30 days, so build your long runs gradually.
  • Fill most of your training with an easy, conversational pace, and add goal pace only in part of the second half of long runs.
  • Pain on bone, like the shin or top of the foot, that hurts when pressed and worsens as you run means rest first; if it's unchanged after a few days of rest, see a doctor.
