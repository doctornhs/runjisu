Preparing without injury

  • When preparing for a marathon, injuries happen in training more than on race day. A cohort study found overuse injuries rise when a single run goes more than 10% beyond your longest run of the last 30 days, so build your long runs gradually.
  • Fill most of your training with an easy, conversational pace, and add goal pace only in part of the second half of long runs.
  • Pain on bone, like the shin or top of the foot, that hurts when pressed and worsens as you run means rest first; if it's unchanged after a few days of rest, see a doctor.

Frequently asked questions

What pace per km is Marathon Sub-3:30?

4:58 per km. Run it evenly and you'll finish in 3:29:34. On a 400 m track, that's 1:59 per lap.

What speed should I set on a treadmill?

12.1 km/h or faster. With no wind resistance, a treadmill can feel a little easier than outdoors at the same speed.

For a Marathon Sub-3:30 goal, what 10K time should I be able to run?

At the same aerobic level: 5K about 22:00, 10K about 45:37, Half about 1:41:06. But even with good short-race times, your pace will fade late in the race if your long runs fall short.
