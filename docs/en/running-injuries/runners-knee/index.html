Patellofemoral pain syndrome

Where it hurts · Front of the knee, around the kneecap · Also called Runner's knee

A dull ache around or behind the kneecap, the most common knee pain in runners. It develops when the joint between the kneecap and thighbone takes more repeated load than it can handle.

If it feels like this

  • A broad ache at the front of the knee that's hard to pin to one spot.
  • Worse climbing or descending stairs, squatting and running downhill.
  • It hurts when you stand after sitting a long time with bent knees, as in a cinema or car.

Why it happens

  • Common after quickly increasing running distance or frequency.
  • Weak thigh and hip muscles increase the load on the kneecap joint.
  • Downhills, stairs and deep squats put high pressure on this joint.

What you can do yourself

  • Cut back on aggravating movements (downhills, repeated stairs, deep squats) and run mostly on flat ground.
  • If pain while running goes beyond mild, cut back for the day.
  • Keep up thigh and hip strengthening within a pain-free range.
  • Shortening your stride a little and taking more steps per minute often reduces load on the knee.

See a doctor if

  • Your knee is visibly swollen or warm
  • Your knee suddenly gives way, or catches and won't straighten
  • Pain began after a twist or fall, or hasn't changed after more than 4 weeks of adjusting

This guide is general information and does not replace a diagnosis. Pain in the same spot can have different causes, so see a doctor if symptoms are clear or last.
