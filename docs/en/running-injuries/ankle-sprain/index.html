Ankle sprain

Where it hurts · Outside of the ankle · Also called Rolled ankle

An injury to the outer ligaments when the ankle rolls inward on uneven ground or a curb. Brushing it off as common often leads to an ankle that keeps rolling, so balance training after the pain settles matters.

If it feels like this

  • Right after the sprain, the front and bottom of the outer ankle bone swell and hurt.
  • Bruising may appear within a day or two.
  • It hurts to bear weight and the ankle feels unstable.

Why it happens

  • It happens when you misstep on trails, curbs or dark paths.
  • A previously sprained ankle has poorer balance and rolls again more easily.
  • It often happens late in a run when fatigue has built up.

What you can do yourself

  • Take it easy for the first few days; if swelling is bad, compress it and keep the leg raised.
  • Moving the ankle and bearing weight early, as pain allows, helps recovery.
  • Keep up balance training like single-leg standing for several weeks after the pain settles. It reduces repeat sprains.
  • When single-leg tiptoe standing and hopping in place are pain-free, start running on flat ground.

See a doctor if

  • You can't take four steps, right after the injury or later
  • Pressing the back edge of the ankle bone or the middle bone on the outer foot hurts (a fracture needs to be ruled out)
  • Swelling and instability remain after several weeks

This guide is general information and does not replace a diagnosis. Pain in the same spot can have different causes, so see a doctor if symptoms are clear or last.
