Aerobic fitness is the measure most closely tied to staying healthy and active for life. Even without a race result, you can estimate it from a 12-minute run or a brisk 1.6 km walk.

Why fitness age matters

A Cleveland Clinic study that followed 122,007 adults after treadmill stress tests found that the fitter the group, the lower the risk of death, with no upper limit to the benefit. The fittest group had an 80% lower risk of death than the least fit (bottom 25%).

It's an observational study, so it doesn't mean raising your fitness adds that many years. But unlike age or family history, aerobic fitness is something training can change.

Keeping a body that walks and moves well for a long time is the goal, and running is one of the easiest ways to get there.

To lower your fitness age

  • Frequency first. Three or more times a week, 30 minutes at a conversational pace is the starting point. Mixing walking and running is fine.
  • After a few months, add a weekly session with a few 3–5 minute efforts that leave you breathing hard. It's the most efficient zone for raising aerobic capacity.
  • Retest the same way every 8–12 weeks to see the change.

Before you read the results

  • The comparison uses treadmill stress-test data from 7,783 US adults (FRIEND, 2015). Values for other populations may differ.
  • Values from race results tend to come out lower than lab VO2max. A study comparing 5 km results with measured values found both collegiate and recreational runners came out lower, with a bigger gap in recreational runners. So read fitness age and ranking from race results as "at least this good", especially from half or full marathon results.
  • Race results and the 12-minute run only work when you give it everything. An easy-effort time will make your fitness age look older than it is.
  • The 1.6 km walk equation was built for adults aged 30–69 and has an error of about 4–6. Experienced runners may score low on the walk test, so use a race result instead.
  • If you have chest pain, severe shortness of breath or dizziness, or a heart condition, talk to a medical professional before doing an all-out test.
