on your shoes?

    Check with your eyes, not just the distance

    • Deep creases on the side of the midsole that don't flatten out
    • The outsole rubber is worn through to the midsole
    • Set on a flat surface and viewed from behind, they lean to one side
    • New foot or knee pain on the same route you always run

    How strong is the evidence for replacement rules?

    500–800 km is the rule of thumb used by brands and retailers. Research confirms cushioning declines with distance, but direct evidence that worn shoes raise injury risk is still lacking.

    So this tool treats distance as the point to start checking, not an order to replace. If you're heavier or run mostly on rough surfaces, start checking at the low end of the range. Carbon-plated racing shoes have almost no lifespan research, so we apply the lightweight · racing range.
